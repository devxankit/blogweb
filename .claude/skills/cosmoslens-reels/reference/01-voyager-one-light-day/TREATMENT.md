# CosmosLens Reel 01: "One Light-Day": treatment

Instagram Reel · 1080×1920 vertical · 30 fps · 60 s · two versions from one picture timeline:
**English** and **Hinglish** (Hindi voice read from Devanagari, Roman Hinglish captions). Word-by-word captions burned in, plus an .srt for each.
Post window: 15–18 Nov 2026 (the milestone is 18 Nov 2026).

## Brief

CosmosLens (www.cosmoslens.com) is a space and science site for Indian readers. This first reel is a broad, shareable "wow" piece, not tied to a site story. Subject: on **18 November 2026**, Voyager 1 becomes the first human-made object **one light-day from Earth**. Look: "cinematic real space", a custom look built in code (Canvas 2D, like the Arise ad) from real NASA imagery, with bold kinetic captions and an original score. End card: CosmosLens logo + "link in bio · cosmoslens.com".

## Three candidate structures

1. **Ride the message.** We "send" a message from India and ride the radio pulse outward at light speed while a light-time clock runs at the top of the frame: the Moon in a second, Neptune in four hours, then twenty hours of dark, until the pulse finds Voyager. There it carries our raga; it turns back for its last look at us. Ends on "what would *you* send?". A countdown the viewer can't stop watching, a turn and a payoff.
2. **The life story, 1977 → 2026.** Launch, Jupiter, Saturn, Pale Blue Dot, interstellar space, one light-day. Accurate and familiar, but it is a list, and the hook ("in 1977…") is slow.
3. **Start on the dot.** Open on the Pale Blue Dot ("this pixel is you"), then pull back to Voyager. A strong first frame, but it spends the most famous image in second one and leaves nothing to rise toward.

**Chosen: 1.** The clock is a built-in retention device (people stay to see it hit 24:00:00), the hook is a strange, true claim about something the viewer does every day (send a message), and the ending hands the viewer a question to answer in the comments. It also lets the Pale Blue Dot arrive as the emotional peak, not the opening.

## Logline and arc

A message we send today arrives tomorrow: we ride it across a full light-day to the loneliest machine humans ever built, which is carrying our song, and which once turned around to look at us.

- Setup (0–8 s): the claim, the send, the milestone.
- Development (8–24 s): the ride. Moon, Neptune, then twenty hours of nothing; the clock accelerates.
- Arrival (24–36 s): Voyager. Its age, its power, its song: "Where are you going?" → silence.
- Turn (37–48 s): the look back. The Pale Blue Dot: all of us, smaller than one pixel.
- Ending (48–60 s): "So if your message reaches it tomorrow, what would you send?" End card; the clock resets to 00:00:00, so the reel loops into its own first frame.

## The look: "Gold we send, blue we are"

- **Real space, never invented.** Every celestial body is a real NASA photograph (graded, cut out, animated). Everything else (the pulse, the clock, the orbits, the labels) is drawn in code, thin and precise.
- **Two colours carry the story**, taken from the CosmosLens logo: **gold `#e8b24a`** is what we send (the message pulse, the Golden Record, the CTA); **pale blue `#6f97f4`** is home (Earth, the dot). Everything else is a cool neutral ladder on near-black `#04060b` with a navy lift `#0c1a2e`.
- **Grade.** Earth and the Pale Blue Dot keep their colour; the deep-space ride is desaturated and cold; the Golden Record warms the frame. The grade follows the arc.
- **Type.** Schibsted Grotesk (the site's sans) heavy for captions, numbers and the clock (tabular figures); Newsreader Display Italic (the site's serif) for the two emotional lines ("Where are you going?", "That's us."). Devanagari for जात कहाँ हो in a system Devanagari face.
- **The light-time clock** is the spine: top centre, below the Reels safe zone, `T+ 00:00:00` with a hairline progress bar from Earth to Voyager.
- **Kinetic captions**: 2–4 words at a time, the spoken word lights gold; centred at y ≈ 1330–1460. Safe zones: nothing key in the top 220 px, the bottom 380 px, or the right 140 px (Reels buttons).

## Benchmark

- **"Riding Light" (Alphonse Swinehart, 2012), and NASA SVS light-travel visualisations.** Learn: riding a photon in real time with a clock is gripping by itself; scale is felt through waiting. Don't take: their visuals, music, or framing.
- **"The Farthest" (2017 Voyager documentary).** Learn: let the real archival images carry the emotion; give the Pale Blue Dot silence. Don't take: footage, interviews, music, or lines.
- **High-retention science Shorts and Reels.** Learn: a strange true claim in the first second, one idea per screen, captions as design, a question that earns comments, a loopable end. Don't take: any channel's fonts, colours, characters or catchphrases.

## Cue map

80 BPM, 4/4 (beat 0.75 s, bar 3.0 s, 20 bars). Original score in **raga Bhairavi's scale** on Sa = D (D · E♭ · F · G · A · B♭ · C), the mode of the song on the record, played by orchestral samples and a synthesised tanpura drone. No melody from the original recording is used.

- 0.0–2.4: tanpura drone (Pa–Sa–Sa–Sa) + a low sub; the typing ticks.
- 2.4: **send**: a bright hand-chime D6 + a radio beep; the pulse leaves.
- 3.0–9.0: pizzicato pulse on the beat (cellos + contrabass), low string pad D–E♭ (the komal re is the tension).
- 9.0–16.5 (the ride): cello spiccato eighths + timpani on bar downbeats; a hit for the Moon (10.5) and Neptune (13.9).
- 16.5–24.0 ("twenty hours"): the eighths double into sixteenths, violins tremolo crescendo, bass-drum roll, the clock ticks blur into a whir.
- **24.0 downbeat**: arrival boom (bass drum + low gong + organ pedal D), then everything drops to the drone and a high string pad.
- 27–33: soft pizzicato heartbeat under the stats; harp + glockenspiel shimmer as the record appears (31.5).
- 33–36.3: the solo violin states the first half of the Bhairavi theme… and stops mid-phrase on "Where are you going?".
- **36.3–37.8: silence** (only a faint carrier hiss).
- 37.8: first sound after the silence: the solo cello completes the theme over the tanpura; at 39.0 the whip-pan whoosh; **41.0**: strings + horn swell as the Pale Blue Dot fills the frame.
- **47.6–48.6: near-silence**: one piano D.
- 49.0–58.5: the full theme on violins, horns and piano in octaves; drums on bars.
- 58.5: the last chord (D, Sa) with a gong; the tanpura rings to 60.0 and matches the opening drone, so the loop is seamless.

## Voice

- English: an audition picks the narrator: a calm, deep male or a warm female voice (Kokoro, offline), or Microsoft's Indian-English neural voice (edge-tts, online).
- Hinglish: Microsoft's Hindi neural voice (edge-tts, `hi-IN-MadhurNeural` or `hi-IN-SwaraNeural`), read from Devanagari.
- Every line must pass the Whisper speech-to-text check.

## Captions and titles

- Captions: Schibsted Grotesk 800, 64 px, white with a soft dark shadow (no pill: the space backgrounds are dark), the spoken word in gold, 2–4 words per card.
- Headline numbers: Schibsted 900, 150–220 px, tabular figures.
- Emotional lines: Newsreader Display Italic, 96 px.
- Burned in by default (most Reels are watched muted); an .srt is also delivered.
