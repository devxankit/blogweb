# Facts used in the reel, and where they come from

| Claim | Source or measurement |
|---|---|
| Voyager 1 reaches one light-day from Earth on 18 Nov 2026 | NASA, as reported by EarthSky (https://earthsky.org/space/voyager-1-1-light-day-from-earth-november-17-18-2026/) and IFLScience (https://iflscience.com/its-official-voyager-1-will-cross-the-1-light-day-threshold-on-november-18-2026-82733): 12:16:07 a.m. CST, 18 Nov 2026 (06:16 UTC) |
| Our check of that date | JPL Horizons (target -31, centre 500@399, geometric vectors): Earth–Voyager 1 range crosses 25,902,068,371 km between 10:00 and 12:00 UTC on 18 Nov 2026. That is the same day as NASA's date, a few hours later (different ephemeris or reference point). The reel says only "November 18th" |
| It never comes back within one light-day | **Our calculation**, JPL Horizons at 10-day steps, Nov 2026 – Mar 2028: the minimum after the crossing is 1.00104 light-days (20–30 Apr 2027), then it grows. Used in the post caption |
| 1 light-day = 25.9 billion km | 299,792.458 km/s × 86,400 s = 25,902,068,371 km |
| Light passes the Moon in about a second | 384,400 km ÷ c = 1.28 s |
| Neptune in about four hours | Neptune is ~29–31 AU from Earth: 4.0–4.3 h of light time |
| ~61,000 km/h | JPL Horizons heliocentric speed, 18 Nov 2026: 16.88 km/s = 60,770 km/h |
| Launched in 1977 | 5 Sep 1977 (NASA/JPL) |
| ~22 W transmitter, less than a fridge bulb | JPL's description of the transmitter as ~22–23 W, and the fridge-bulb comparison, as reported (https://maketecheasier.com/voyager-1-signal-power-distance/) |
| "Jaat Kahan Ho" by Kesarbai Kerkar (raga Bhairavi) is on the Golden Record | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kesarbai_Kerkar and the Golden Record track list; the title asks "where are you going?" |
| Pale Blue Dot: 14 Feb 1990, ~6 billion km, cameras then switched off for good, Earth ≈ 0.12 px | NASA (https://science.nasa.gov/mission/voyager/voyager-1s-pale-blue-dot) |
| "More than four times farther now" (not in the reel; available for the caption) | 40.5 AU (Feb 1990) vs 173.1 AU (Nov 2026) = 4.3× |
