# The reel format

These are what Instagram's ranking appears to reward, based on 2026 marketing guides. Their numbers conflict, so treat this as patterns, not rules:

- **The first 1–3 seconds** decide whether people keep watching.
- **Completion rate** matters.
- **Sends (DM shares)** carry the most weight.
- **Original audio** helps.
- **Many viewers watch muted**, so burn the captions in.

## The 60-second shape (reel 01's timing, at 80 BPM, bar = 3 s)

| Time | Beat | Job |
|---|---|---|
| 0–4 s | **Hook** | A strange, true claim about something the viewer does ("Send a message to Voyager 1 today, and it won't arrive until tomorrow"). The caption is on screen from frame 0, and the first frame works as a thumbnail |
| 4–10 s | **The stake** | Why now: the date or milestone, as one giant number card ("1 LIGHT-DAY = 25.9 BILLION KM · 18 NOV 2026") |
| 10–24 s | **The ride** | A retention device the viewer can't stop watching: a running clock, a countdown, a race, a scale zoom. Landmarks every 3–4 s; accelerate into the turn |
| 24 s | **The turn** | A hit on a bar downbeat: arrival, reveal, contradiction |
| 24–36 s | **Development** | 2–3 facts on the beat as stat lines, then the emotional or India angle (reel 01: the raga on the Golden Record) |
| ~36.5 s | **Silence 1** | A real silence (1–1.5 s). The first sound after it is one of the most important in the reel |
| 38–48 s | **The peak** | The payoff image, real (reel 01: the Pale Blue Dot photo, pushed in to the pixel) |
| ~47.9 s | **Silence 2** | A near-silence: one note |
| 49–55 s | **The question** | Turn it back to the viewer in big type ("WHAT WOULD YOU SEND?"), with a comment prompt and an empty message bubble. Questions earn comments; comments and sends earn reach |
| 55–60 s | **End card** | Logo, "CosmosLens", "The sky, explained.", "Follow for more space stories", "link in bio · cosmoslens.com". Hold for at least 4 s. The last 0.9 s crossfades into frame 0, so the reel loops |

## Writing

- **About 120 spoken words per minute,** in short lines with one idea per line. Spell numbers out in `text` for the voice and use digits in `sub` and `roman` for the captions.
- **Concrete comparisons:** "less than a fridge bulb", "smaller than one pixel", "in about a second".
- **India angle where it's true:** Indian cities, ISRO, Indian music, the sky from India. Never invent one.
- **Hinglish:** natural spoken Hindi with English nouns (message, light-day, record, pixel). The voice reads Devanagari; captions show Roman.

## Picture rules

- **Safe zones:** nothing key in the top 220 px (the clock or HUD sits at about 250–400), the bottom 380 px (captions sit at y ≈ 1400, or 1480 when the middle is busy), or the right 140 px.
- **Captions:** Schibsted 800, 68 px, upper case, 1–4 words per card, the spoken word in gold, a soft shadow and no box.
- **One idea per screen.** Subjects fill at least a third of the frame height at key moments.
- **Circle grammar:** ring, pulse, record, porthole. Repeat one shape language across transitions.
- **At least four camera moves** (push, pull, track, whip, orbit, Ken Burns), and **one signature shot.**

## Sound rules

- **Three layers:** ambience (a deep-space rumble, never a "room"), foley for every action, and music in the topic's colour.
- **Two silences** (above). Music ducks about 6 dB under the voice, which sits about 7 dB above the music's RMS.
- **The final chord** sits on a bar about 1.5 s before the end. The drone or tail matches the opening, so the loop is seamless.
